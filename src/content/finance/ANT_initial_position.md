29 Oct 2026 - **Anthropic** filed its S-1 with the SEC on 26th October. As expected from an S-1, the 318-page-long document consists of a prospectus which includes Management’s Discussion and Analysis (MD&A), financial statements (FS), audit opinions, and footnotes among other stuff. The following article is the product of an analysis of the provided material. It questions whether the company would be able to meet the financial requirements it has agreed to based on the limited material provided; The material is unable to convince the analyst. 

# Preliminary Comment on Anthropic's Financials and Statements

[Anthropic's financial statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm#ica694c859b8e458dbf6a73f56dde9d42_1611) set up an intriguing picture of the business. The company provides consolidated statements for the years 2023, 2024, 2025, and the year to date(YTD) statements of 2026. For a year-on-year compariosn, the company also provides statements for the first 6 months of 2025. The 2025TD and 2026YTD are unaudited statements. Management also provides a quarterly income statement covering 2025 and 2026 in its analysis.

The Income statement shows a business incurring substantial losses, both due to operations and its other activities, despite having an exceptional revenue growth. Compared to 2023, the company has earned almost 400 times more revenue in the first six months of 2026, showing \$16.2 billion. Yet, has an operating margin of -4%. # The Question

Management states that with the resulting liquidity from the financing, it plans to invest into data center infrastructure, research, model development, serving customers, and internal usage of Claude. They expect compute to continue remaining their largest operating expense while personnel expense continues to increase in absolute dollars. 

Till now, Anthropic has been using services provided by cloud service providers (CSPs) to outsource the computing required to run its models. This turn towards constructing their own data center infrastructure is to "support anticipated demand for our intelligence service. Given the long lead times between contracting for, purchasing, or leasing compute and that compute available to serve workloads, we secure the arrangements well in advance of anticipated demand".
*Source: Anthropic, PBC, Form S-1, MD&A, Liquidity and Capital Resources.*

The current demand that the company is experiencing has driven revenue upward from \$1.16 billion dollars in the first six months of 2025 to \$16.29 billion in 2026YTD. Most of this growth comes from consumption based revenue (contributing \$12.1 billion). A similar trend was seen in the revenue growth from \$0.386 billion in 2024 to \$4.2 billion in 2025, with consumption based revenue contributing \$3.5 billion in the growth.

Thus, the spectacular growth experienced by Anthropic comes from an increasing consumption-based revenue. To meet the anticipated consumption demands of the future, Anthropic has entered into infrastructure service agreements with several companies. The details of which are in the following table:

Ignoring the SpaceX commitment, using a straight-line method, The analyst estimates with an estimate of \$9.11 billion quarterly expense imposed on the company because of the agreements mentioned above.

On top of this, the company expects for the personnel expenses to increase implying an increase in stock compensation. Hence overall, in the future years, the company expects to do large capex, increase stock compensation, and have its operating expenses to be atleast around a figure of \$9.11 billion, while current revenue is barely above \$16 billion dollars. 

This raises the question, being an operational intensive business, will Anthropic be able to meet its future requirements as they show up i.e. a solvency issue arises.

We therefore argue why the provided material is not sufficient enough to convince the analyst that it will.

# Arguments for Insufficient Conviction

As seen, there lies a substantial amount of future requirements on the company. The analyst wonders how might the company be able to meet these or even what measures can be taken to hedge the risk of not meeting them.

As of now, the company is in a revolving credit agreement with certain lenders and Morgan Stanley, which allows them a \$15 billion senior secured revolving facility which matures on September 4, 2031. It is expected that the company might use this. Yet the agreement comes with the clause that the agreement is "subject to earlier springing maturity if more than \$500 million in aggregate scheduled cash principal payments on certain of our indebtedness are required within 91-days preceeding the date on which any such scheduled principal payment is do". Hence in the case that the company might be defaulting on one of the payments, It is hard to be assured that the credit agreement might help the company.

Another argument for the company meeting its agreements would be pointing towards its spectacular revenue growth which has resulted into operating profit being positive over the last quarter. Keeping this spectacular growth as a base is very unstable. The reason being, it isn't expected that the company would be able to maintain such a growth rate consistently. Further, currently the revenue is majorly of the form of consumer based revenue. Since these consumers are not as such incentivised to remain loyal customers to the company, This raises the question, how does the company plan to keep them as customers? Currently, the management belives taht the growth is being driven because of their superior model and research, hence how does the company ensure to maintain and grow that superiority? Providing stock compensation to employees will increase operating expense hence eating from the profits and further diluting the common share base. Further, in assuming that there would be future growth of the company, there is an implicit assumption, that more customers will demand services from the company, I do not see why that demand is most likely to happen.

The company might use the current liquidity to fund itself through the agreements while its own infrastructure is constructed. This accompanied with promising reduction in compute costs as a percentage of revenue might form a stronger argument for the company. The major decrease in compute margins is amazing for the company, yet it is to be noted that the company has only started making positive gross profits only in the last quarter. It is still to show that the company is capable of self financing itself through operations. Hence any funds that are expensed away from the company, do not act in making the company self sufficient. With expectation of rising costs, I believe it must be on the forefront of the company to show its self-sufficiency. 

Thus without showing any reliable way of increasing revenue, or reducing expenses, or a way of decreasing dependence on external financing so that it can face it's agreements, particularly in the short term, the company fails to convince me that it will be able to meet its future financial agreements.
