29 Oct 2026 - **Anthropic** filed its S-1 with the SEC on 26th October. As expected from an S-1, the 318-page-long document consists of a prospectus which includes Management’s Discussion and Analysis (MD&A), financial statements (FS), audit opinions, and footnotes among other stuff. The following article is the product of an analysis of the provided material. It questions whether the company would be able to meet the financial requirements it has agreed to based on the limited material provided; The material is unable to convince the analyst. 

# Introduction

The following article is an excercise to develop a defensible investment position and recommendation when it comes to investing in particular securities. The excercise is purely educational and is not be taken as an investment advice, despite its structure. In this case, the particular security being discussed is the heated upcoming IPO by Anthropic. In the larger process of the excercise, I would seek the answers to three questions. The first questioning if the business is self sustainable. The second questions if the business can self sustain then  can the buisness generate a profit for an investor. The final question being if the business can generate a profit for the investor then what is the ideal present value of those profits. On examining Anthropic, using the information provided in its S-1, I do not find convincing evidence that, at the current moment, the company is self sustainable. The following is my argument, my current position and recommendation towards investing in the security and what further evidence I require to change the position.

I first provide a quick summary of Anthropic's financials, then point out the tension between its future agreements and current standing. Then, I elaborate on why I remain unconvinced despite the existence of some reasonale ways to resolve that tension and finally, ending with my recommendation and evidence required to convince me.

# The Question

Management states that with the resulting liquidity from financing, it plans to invest in data center infrastructure, research, model development, serving customers, and internal usage of Claude. They expect compute to continue remaining their largest operating expense while personnel expense continues to increase in absolute dollars. 

Till now, Anthropic has been using services provided by cloud service providers (CSPs) to outsource the computing required to run its models. This turn towards constructing their own data center infrastructure is to "support anticipated demand for our intelligence service. Given the long lead times between contracting for, purchasing, or leasing compute and that compute available to serve workloads, we secure the arrangements well in advance of anticipated demand".
*Source: Anthropic, PBC, Form S-1, MD&A, Liquidity and Capital Resources.*

The current demand that the company is experiencing has driven revenue upward from \$1.16 billion dollars in the first six months of 2025 to \$16.29 billion in 2026YTD. Most of this growth comes from consumption based revenue (contributing \$12.1 billion). A similar trend was seen in the revenue growth from \$0.386 billion in 2024 to \$4.2 billion in 2025, with consumption based revenue contributing \$3.5 billion in the growth.

Thus, the spectacular growth experienced by Anthropic comes from an increasing consumption-based revenue. To meet the anticipated consumption demands of the future, Anthropic has entered into infrastructure service agreements with several companies. The details of which are in the following table:

This estimate is intended to illustrate the scale of the commitments rather than represent the actual quarterly payment schedule.

On top of this, the company expects for the personnel expenses to increase implying an increase in stock compensation. Hence overall, in the future years, the company expects to do large capex, increase stock compensation, and have its expenses to be atleast around a figure of \$9.11 billion, while revenue barely meets the required costs of operating i.e. while teh company has shown only very little signs of being able to sustain itself without financing.

This raises the question, considering it's starting point where teh company is barely able to cover its operational expenses along with the added pressure of the agreements and planned capex, would the company be able to self finance itself through operations in the short term?

I therefore argue why the provided material is not sufficient enough to convince me that it will.

# Arguments for Insufficient Conviction

As seen, there lies a substantial amount of future requirements on the company. Thus, I wonder reasonable ways through which the company might resolve the pressure or hedge the risk of not meeting the obligations.

As of now, the company is in a revolving credit agreement with certain lenders and Morgan Stanley, which allows them a \$15 billion senior secured revolving facility which matures on September 4, 2031. It is expected that the company might use this. Yet the agreement comes with the clause that the agreement is "subject to earlier springing maturity if more than \$500 million in aggregate scheduled cash principal payments on certain of our indebtedness are required within 91-days preceeding the date on which any such scheduled principal payment is do". Hence in the case that the company might be defaulting on one of the payments, It is hard to be assured that the credit agreement might help the company. Therefore, I am not satisfied by just relying on the credit arrangement as a hedge.

Another strong argument for the company meeting its agreements would be pointing towards its spectacular revenue growth which has resulted into operating profit being positive over the last quarter. Keeping this spectacular growth as a base is still unconvincing. The reason being, it isn't shown that the company would be able to maintain such a growth rate consistently for a long enough period of time. Current performance of a company is not indicative of the future, it may provide a general direction yet does not predict it. Therefore, I would require further statsitcs to show that most likely the future demand will grow fast enough and remain consistent enough.

 Further, currently the revenue is majorly of the form of consumer based revenue. Since these consumers are not as such incentivised to remain loyal customers to the company, This raises the question, how does the company plan to keep them as customers? Currently, the management belives taht the growth is being driven because of their superior model and research, hence how does the company ensure to maintain and grow that superiority? Providing stock compensation to employees will increase operating expense hence eating from the profits and further diluting the common share base. These problems are foundational to the expected revenue growth. I haven't seen any comment from the management regarding actiosn to handle these problems, particullarly the stock compensation. In contrast, the management expects to increase compensation. therefore, I remain unconvinced. 

Another reasonable way might be the company using the current liquidity to fund itself through the agreements while its own infrastructure is constructed. This accompanied with promising reduction in compute costs as a percentage of revenue might form a stronger argument for the company. The major decrease in compute margins is amazing for the company, yet it is to be noted that the company has only started making positive gross profits only in the last quarter. Net profits still remain negative. It is still to show that the company is capable of self financing itself through operations. Hence any funds that are expensed away from the company, do not act in making the company self sufficient. With expectation of rising costs, I believe it must be on the forefront of the company to show its self-sufficiency. Therefore, again I remain unconvinced by the argument taht the company might just survive through the short term using its accumulated liquidity. 

Hence, I have not been able to see any reasonable way through which the company may become self sufficient while also meeting the future agreements. The future agreements only put further pressure onto the company to grow revenue faster and reduce expenses faster, while starting from a position where its operations are barely able to keep it going. That is a long leap based on assumptions which I have scrutinised and demand some form of basis for. Hence, I remain unconvinced that based on it's current position and the added pressure, that anthropic would be able to self sustain itself through operations in the short term.

# Final Recommendation and Position

Hence, with respect to this IPO, I place myself as follows:

Based on the provided evidence in its S-1, I would not recommend to invest in the upcoming IPO for the reason, that I remain unconvinced that based on its current position and direction, along with the added finnancial obligations from its agreements, the business would be able to finance itself sufficiently through operations in the short term. Atleast not before it completes construction of its own infrastructure, as planned by the management. The management assumes substantial future demand in justifying its large development and financing requirments. I have not encountered sufficiently strong evidence implying that the future demand exists and even more, that the current and future demand is essential and consistent i.e. it won't vanish overnight because of unforseen reasons. If any such data comes to light, I would reconsider my position. I would also reconsider it if I have reasons to believe enough reduction of expenses, or any other knowledge that directly concering the tension I raised above and self sufficieny of the company. This may include financial publications in the future, management comments, Industrial paper and other sources. Till then, I stick to the position and only plan to follow up, if there is are material developments that affect the company's financial outlook.
