# On Taco bell and nutrition

A part of my commute occasionally consists of going through the food court at a train station while awaiting my train's arrival. During one such occasions, I observed a muscualr man eating a box of fries from taco bell. 

From general sources and science that was taught to me in school, I know that food from taco bell especially the one that contains cheese is filled with fat which contributes to obesity in people. Yet, I would like to entertain a ridiculous claim: The man is muscular because he eats taco bell, and see how a *nyAyikA* would go about trying to argue for or against the claim. 

Following the tradition, I first set out a reason why I investigate the claim. I desire to be muscular like the man, therefore I investigate how to be like that and hence what should I consume to be muscular like him?

I recall the canonicall *devadatta* argument regarding *arthApati*: Suppose devadatta is fat and he doesn't eat during the day, therefore he must eat during the night. Where *mImAmamsA* cosniders *arthApati* as a distinct source of knowledge, *nyAya* argues that it is just an inference. I do not touch that argument, and accept the *nyAYa* stance. 

Therefore, intuitivelly, I propose an analogus inference as: The man is muscualr and I have seen him only eat Taco Bell, therefore he must be muscular because of Taco Bell. 

Immediately, objections come to the surface regarding the proposed argument. the first one being why am I even allowed to replace the premises in the *devadatta* argument with my own premiesis i.e. why is the same inferential chain still valid with my premises?

The first premise in teh *devadatta* argument is that "*devadatta* is fat", and my first premise is "that man is muscular". Hence the equivalency of the premises is immediately clear, as I have just replaced the nouns *devadatta* and fat with the nouns "that man" and "muscualr" respectively. Therefore the replacement works in teh first premise. 

The second premise in the devadatta argument is that "*devadatta* does not eat during the day". My second premise is "I have seen that man eat only taco bell". The equivalency for this premise isn't immediately clear. Consider the premise "*devadatta* does not eat during the day". It is clear that the premise is the same as "I know that *devadatta* only eats during the day". Now I take my premise and show how it is equivalent to this premise. Consider the premise "I have seen that man eat only taco bell", hence from this I immediately know that the man eats taco bell. Now I ask does he **only** eat taco bell? 

I use *tarka* to answer this. Suppose he eats other food. If he eats other food and they end his hunger then he won't eat taco bell. He is eating taco bell, therefore either he doesn't eat other food or other food don't full fill him. Yet, other foods are full filling, when I eat other food, my hunger ends. Is there a specific reason why they might not fulfill specifically him? I see no reason why. Therefore, it must be that he doesn't eat other food. 

An objection to this tarka would be, he usually eats other food and is only eating taco bell now. To that, I would ask how is it known that he usually eats other food? If the answer is that people don't usually eat taco bell for every meal, I would say that might not apply to him, because I am seeing him eat taco bell right now. Hence he definitely eats taco bell and I have not seen him eat anything else, hence I cannot say that he eats other food. Knowing that he is eating taco bell, it makes me infer that there is a time when he eats taco bell, but I have no evidence to say that there is a time where he eats other food, hence because of lack of evidence for other foods, I could only say that he eats taco bell all the time even though people don't usually eat taco bell all the time. 

Hence from this I show that the premise "I have seen that man eat only taco bell" can be replaced with "I know that that man eats only taco bell". Further there is a bigger leap that we have to make. The original premise in teh *devadatta* argument makes a comment on what time *devadatta* eats, whereas my premise makes a comment on what that man eats. Both the premises are comments on someone's eating habbits. Hence is it neccesaary that the comment has to be on time?
